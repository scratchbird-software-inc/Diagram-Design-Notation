Data structures and meaning

Dataset lineage and derivation

Where each dataset comes from: sources, transformations and the datasets they produce, end to end.

Why use it?

Every number on a report should answer "from where" in one look.

When it helps

Auditing data products, debugging a wrong number, or onboarding to a pipeline.

How to read it

  • Read right-to-left from the report: each arrow is a derivation step you can inspect.
  • A dataset with no recorded source is an assumption, not a fact.

What you need

  • The sources
  • The transformation steps
  • The produced datasets

Things to check

  • Do not skip the boring intermediate datasets — they are where errors hide.
  • Do not draw lineage at a granularity you cannot actually inspect.
Scope of this diagram

Declared lineage; no data actually flows when you read this.

Work with the converted example

  1. Open the viewer and select diagram. Inspect the rendered view and its source.
  2. Open the designer. Keep the selected profile and inspect its legal palette and property controls.
  3. Change one displayed title or an appropriate data value. Check the live update in the resulting diagram.
  4. Run the verifier and review its diagnostics before moving complete content into review.
  5. Download your edited DDN source to retain the change.

The example uses the indentation-based ddn-next-1 dialect. The language guide explains reusable definitions, dotted data rows and explicit property blocks.
