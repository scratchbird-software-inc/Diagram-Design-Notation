UML activity — pins, decisions and interrupts

Actions wired by object and control flow: pins as typed ports on actions, decision/merge diamonds, fork/join bars, interruptible regions.

uml.activity@2New DDN source
Open viewerOpen designerRun verifierDownload DDN

Why use it?

The activity diagram is where "the process" becomes checkable: every input must come from somewhere.

When it helps

Detailing an algorithm or business process one level below a use case.

How to read it

  • Rounded boxes are actions; small squares on their edges are pins (typed inputs/outputs).
  • Diamonds decide or merge; thick bars fork and rejoin concurrent flow.

What you need

  • The actions
  • What each consumes and produces
  • The decision logic

Things to check

  • Do not leave a required pin unfed — the validator treats it as the defect it is.
  • Merge and decision are different diamonds; do not swap them.
Scope of this diagram

Declared flow with pin and region validation; no execution or simulation is performed.

Work with the converted example

  1. Open the viewer and select pins. Inspect the rendered view and its source.
  2. Open the designer. Keep the selected profile and inspect its legal palette and property controls.
  3. Change one displayed title or an appropriate data value. Check the live update in the resulting diagram.
  4. Run the verifier and review its diagnostics before moving complete content into review.
  5. Download your edited DDN source to retain the change.

The example uses the indentation-based ddn-next-1 dialect. The language guide explains reusable definitions, dotted data rows and explicit property blocks.
