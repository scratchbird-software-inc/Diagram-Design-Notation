BPMN choreography

Interactions as first-class tasks: each choreography task names the initiating and receiving participants in its bands.

bpmn.choreography@1New DDN source
Open viewerOpen designerRun verifierDownload DDN

Why use it?

When no party owns the process, the contract is the sequence of exchanges itself — the choreography draws exactly that.

When it helps

Cross-organisation protocols: ordering, settlement, any dance with no conductor.

How to read it

  • Each task is one exchange; the shaded band is the receiver, the light band initiates.
  • Sequence flow orders the exchanges, not internal steps — internals live in each party's own process.

What you need

  • The participants
  • The ordered exchanges
  • Who initiates each one

Things to check

  • Do not model one party's internal steps here — that is a process diagram's job.
  • The initiator band must be the sender of the first message in the exchange.
Scope of this diagram

Declared choreography with participant-band validation; enforcement between organisations is out of scope.

Work with the converted example

  1. Open the viewer and select choreography. Inspect the rendered view and its source.
  2. Open the designer. Keep the selected profile and inspect its legal palette and property controls.
  3. Change one displayed title or an appropriate data value. Check the live update in the resulting diagram.
  4. Run the verifier and review its diagnostics before moving complete content into review.
  5. Download your edited DDN source to retain the change.

The example uses the indentation-based ddn-next-1 dialect. The language guide explains reusable definitions, dotted data rows and explicit property blocks.
