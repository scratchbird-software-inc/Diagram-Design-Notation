What this diagram shows

Interactions as first-class tasks: each choreography task names the initiating and receiving participants in its bands.

Why use it?

When no party owns the process, the contract is the sequence of exchanges itself — the choreography draws exactly that.

When is it useful?

Cross-organisation protocols: ordering, settlement, any dance with no conductor.

How to read this example

  1. Each task is one exchange; the shaded band is the receiver, the light band initiates.
  2. Sequence flow orders the exchanges, not internal steps — internals live in each party's own process.

Information to gather first

  • The participants
  • The ordered exchanges
  • Who initiates each one

Boundaries and common mistakes

Declared choreography with participant-band validation; enforcement between organisations is out of scope.

  • Do not model one party's internal steps here — that is a process diagram's job.
  • The initiator band must be the sender of the first message in the exchange.
