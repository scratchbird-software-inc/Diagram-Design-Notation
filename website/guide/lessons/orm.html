ORM fact model

Object-Role Modeling: entity types, value types and the fact roles between them, with uniqueness and mandatory constraints drawn on the roles.

orm.basic@1New DDN source
Open viewerOpen designerRun verifierDownload DDN

Why use it?

ORM verbalises facts in sentences non-modellers can confirm, and the constraints are drawn where they apply.

When it helps

Deep domain modelling where every constraint must survive a business review.

How to read it

  • Ellipses are object types; boxes on the fact lines are roles — read a fact as a sentence across the roles.
  • Bars over roles are uniqueness constraints; dots are mandatory — each is a separate, checkable claim.

What you need

  • The fact types as sentences
  • The object and value types
  • The constraints per role

Things to check

  • Do not model a ternary fact as two binaries — the constraint semantics differ.
  • A role with no uniqueness reading is an unfinished sentence.
Scope of this diagram

Declared fact model with constraint validation; schema generation is a separate step, not claimed here.

Work with the converted example

  1. Open the viewer and select model. Inspect the rendered view and its source.
  2. Open the designer. Keep the selected profile and inspect its legal palette and property controls.
  3. Change one displayed title or an appropriate data value. Check the live update in the resulting diagram.
  4. Run the verifier and review its diagnostics before moving complete content into review.
  5. Download your edited DDN source to retain the change.

The example uses the indentation-based ddn-next-1 dialect. The language guide explains reusable definitions, dotted data rows and explicit property blocks.
