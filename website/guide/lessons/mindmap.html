Hierarchies, maps and symbol libraries

Mind map

A radial hierarchy: one central topic, branches of subtopics growing outward.

mindmap.basic@1New DDN source
Open viewerOpen designerRun verifierDownload DDN

Why use it?

Brainstorming needs a shape that rewards adding, not arranging — the map grows the way the conversation does.

When it helps

Workshops, note-taking, scope exploration before any formal structure exists.

How to read it

  • The centre is the subject; each ring outward is one level more specific.
  • Sibling order is the order they were declared, not a ranking.
  • Branches always leave the vertical centre of an entity's left or right side — the root branches from both.
  • Entities with long note bodies cap at ten rows: a scrollbar pans them, and the lower-right handle resizes the window (presentation only).

What you need

  • The central topic
  • The branches

Things to check

  • Do not force a mind map to hold cross-links — that is a concept map.
  • More than three levels usually means one branch deserves its own map.
Scope of this diagram

Declared hierarchy rendered radially; no semantics beyond parent/child.

Work with the converted example

  1. Open the viewer and select mindmap. Inspect the rendered view and its source.
  2. Open the designer. Keep the selected profile and inspect its legal palette and property controls.
  3. Change one displayed title or an appropriate data value. Check the live update in the resulting diagram.
  4. Run the verifier and review its diagnostics before moving complete content into review.
  5. Download your edited DDN source to retain the change.

The example uses the indentation-based ddn-next-1 dialect. The language guide explains reusable definitions, dotted data rows and explicit property blocks.
