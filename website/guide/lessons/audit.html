What this diagram shows

The governance gates an external party imposes, drawn with the evidence each gate demands before passage.

Why use it?

Audit surprises are usually known gates nobody drew; the diagram makes the evidence list a design input.

When is it useful?

Regulated delivery: planning for audits, certifications, external sign-offs.

How to read this example

  1. Gates are the checkpoints; their attached notes list the required evidence.
  2. Read the order — evidence produced out of order is evidence produced twice.

Information to gather first

  • The external gates
  • The evidence each requires
  • The order they fall due

Boundaries and common mistakes

Declared gates and evidence lists; compliance itself is an audit outcome, not a render outcome.

  • Do not paraphrase a regulator's requirement — quote it and trace your artefact to it.
  • A gate with no owner produces its evidence at the deadline, badly.
