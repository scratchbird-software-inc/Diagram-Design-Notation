Time, composition and linked documentation

Roadmap timeline — phases on one axis

A programme roadmap: phases as bars on one time axis, the gating dependencies between them declared.

Why use it?

A roadmap is the schedule at phase granularity — sequence and overlap are the argument, task tracking lives elsewhere.

When it helps

Programme communication: steering packs, planning baselines, dependency-aware phasing.

How to read it

  • Each bar is a phase with declared start and end dates; arrows are the finish-to-start gates.
  • Overlap between bars is a declared choice — find the gate that still holds across it.

What you need

  • The phases with start/end dates
  • The gating dependencies
  • The status per phase

Things to check

  • Do not track tasks on the roadmap — one bar per phase, or it stops being one.
  • A phase with no gate in or out is either the boundary of the programme or a missing dependency.
Scope of this diagram

Declared dates and dependencies (finish-to-start validated); capacity and criticality analysis belong to the PERT chapter.

Work with the converted example

  1. Open the viewer and select roadmap. Inspect the rendered view and its source.
  2. Open the designer. Keep the selected profile and inspect its legal palette and property controls.
  3. Change one displayed title or an appropriate data value. Check the live update in the resulting diagram.
  4. Run the verifier and review its diagnostics before moving complete content into review.
  5. Download your edited DDN source to retain the change.

The example uses the indentation-based ddn-next-1 dialect. The language guide explains reusable definitions, dotted data rows and explicit property blocks.
