Techniques

Presentation diagrams — illustration art with connection points

The slide-deck idiom: detailed illustrations (people, buildings, servers, vehicles) instead of stroke icons, with relations glued to the artwork’s declared connection anchors.

ddn@1New DDN source
Open viewerOpen designerRun verifierDownload DDN

Why use it?

Stakeholder communication reads pictures faster than notation; an art pack gives the deck idiom without giving up a real model underneath.

When it helps

Executive overviews, sales and training material, any audience that should see a factory, not a rect labelled factory.

How to read it

  • Separate the semantic role from its optional illustration.
  • Connections still bind declared definitions and members.
  • Review missing-asset diagnostics instead of treating a placeholder as the intended illustration.

What you need

  • The model
  • A registered art pack (CLI --pack or registerArtPack)
  • x_art per node

Things to check

  • Art is presentation: without the pack registered, every node draws an honest placeholder — never fake the artwork inline.
  • Do not put fields on art nodes; the illustration owns the body area.
Scope of this diagram

Host-registered packs only (never inlined into the tools); 64 KiB per item; anchors are the four side midpoints in the shipped pack.

Work with the converted example

  1. Open the viewer and select overview. Inspect the rendered view and its source.
  2. Open the designer. Keep the selected profile and inspect its legal palette and property controls.
  3. Change one displayed title or an appropriate data value. Check the live update in the resulting diagram.
  4. Run the verifier and review its diagnostics before moving complete content into review.
  5. Download your edited DDN source to retain the change.

The example uses the indentation-based ddn-next-1 dialect. The language guide explains reusable definitions, dotted data rows and explicit property blocks.
