Techniques

Night and low-light palettes

The same sheet rendered in the night and dark themes across looks — palettes built for projection and low-light rooms.

ddn@1New DDN source
Open viewerOpen designerRun verifierDownload DDN

Why use it?

A diagram projected in a dark room inverts its reading: the night palette keeps contrast where the eye expects it.

When it helps

Ops-room displays, evening reviews, any projected reading in low light.

How to read it

  • Switch variants: the model is identical; ink, surface and accent move to the night scale.
  • Contrast pairs stay deliberate — night is a designed palette, not an inverted filter.

What you need

  • The model
  • The theme declaration on the view

Things to check

  • Do not screenshot a day palette into a night deck — declare the theme and re-render.
  • Theme is view-level; a mixed-theme document is a deliberate choice, say so.
Scope of this diagram

The built-in theme scales (default, neutral, dark, night, forest, base); custom palettes are a publication-format concern.

Work with the converted example

  1. Open the viewer and select classic_night. Inspect the rendered view and its source.
  2. Open the designer. Keep the selected profile and inspect its legal palette and property controls.
  3. Change one displayed title or an appropriate data value. Check the live update in the resulting diagram.
  4. Run the verifier and review its diagnostics before moving complete content into review.
  5. Download your edited DDN source to retain the change.

The example uses the indentation-based ddn-next-1 dialect. The language guide explains reusable definitions, dotted data rows and explicit property blocks.
