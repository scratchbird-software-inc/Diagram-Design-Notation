Charts, distributions and measurement

Distribution chart pack

The distribution family beyond the histogram: violin shapes, density curves, Q-Q plots and quantile dots from one record set.

Why use it?

Shape questions need shape tools — each of these shows what averages and even histograms smooth away.

When it helps

Comparing distributions, checking normality, reading tails.

How to read it

  • The violin mirrors the density; the Q-Q compares against the theoretical line.
  • Quantile dots are records, never a smoothed invention.

What you need

  • The values
  • The comparison target (normal, or another group)
  • The variant that answers your question

Things to check

  • Do not read a KDE bandwidth as data.
  • Do not conclude normality from a pretty Q-Q alone.
Scope of this diagram

Declared kernels and quantiles from the records; no significance testing.

Work with the converted example

  1. Open the viewer and select violin. Inspect the rendered view and its source.
  2. Open the designer. Keep the selected profile and inspect its legal palette and property controls.
  3. Change one displayed title or an appropriate data value. Check the live update in the resulting diagram.
  4. Run the verifier and review its diagnostics before moving complete content into review.
  5. Download your edited DDN source to retain the change.

The example uses the indentation-based ddn-next-1 dialect. The language guide explains reusable definitions, dotted data rows and explicit property blocks.
