What this diagram shows

Entities and the relationships between them at business level: names and cardinalities, no storage design.

Why use it?

Agree on the things the business tracks before any schema discussion; the diagram is the vocabulary contract.

When is it useful?

Early domain workshops and any review where business stakeholders must confirm scope without reading SQL.

How to read this example

  1. Entities are nouns; relationships are verbs with cardinality marks.
  2. Participation marks (zero/one/many) are claims about the domain, not constraints enforced here.

Information to gather first

  • The entities the business names
  • How they relate, in business words
  • Rough cardinalities

How to author this view

  1. Open website/examples/use-cases/02-business-erd.ddn and locate view diagram. This selects existing data; it is not a stored SVG.
  2. Inspect the ddn@1 profile and graph projection. Read the bindings before changing any value.
  3. Use the source-file picker or select a diagram element to find its declaration. Edit labels or values deliberately; keep identifiers stable unless all references are updated.
  4. Apply the source, inspect diagnostics, and compare the expected result. Export the SVG only after a successful render.

Boundaries and common mistakes

Conceptual only: no keys, types or indexes are decided by this view.

  • Do not start assigning datatypes here — that is the logical/physical stage.
  • A relationship without a business-readable verb is usually a missing entity.
